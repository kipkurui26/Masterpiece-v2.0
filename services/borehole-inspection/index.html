What You Might Need to Establish About Your Borehole

Sometimes you need to verify something about an existing borehole that cannot be confirmed from the surface or available records. You may need an inspection if you are asking:

  • How deep is the borehole really?
  • What does the casing look like, and are there visible defects?
  • Is something inside the borehole preventing the pump from being lowered?
  • What is the borehole's static water level?
  • Can I see what is actually inside the borehole before deciding whether further work is necessary?
  • Do the existing borehole records match what is actually there?

The inspection is guided by the question you need answered.

What Does the Inspection Involve?

We focus on the specific question you need answered about your borehole and inspect the borehole accordingly. Depending on what you need to establish, the inspection may involve:

01

Checking the Borehole's Depth and Water Level

We establish the actual borehole depth and static water level, where required for the inspection.

02

Examining the Casing

A downhole camera allows us to see the casing and check its profile and visible condition, including any defects or irregularities along the inspected section.

03

Checking for Obstructions

If something is preventing the pump from being lowered, the inspection can help identify and locate the object and provide information that can guide its removal.

04

Recording What We Find

We document the relevant findings in an inspection report and provide supporting video footage from the inspection.

What we establish depends on the borehole, the inspection conditions and what you need to find out.

What a Borehole Inspection Cannot Tell You

A borehole inspection can answer specific questions about the physical condition of your borehole. It cannot, on its own, answer every question about your water supply. For example, an inspection does not establish:

  • How much water the borehole can sustainably produce
  • Whether the water is safe to drink
  • Whether the pump is performing properly
  • Every possible cause of reduced water production

These questions may require other forms of assessment.

For example: Borehole performance and available yield may require pumping tests. Water safety requires laboratory water quality testing. If the problem may involve the pump or other equipment, the pumping system may need to be assessed separately.

An inspection gives you specific information about what was observed inside the borehole. We use those findings to help you understand your options and determine whether further assessment is necessary.

What Happens After the Inspection?

An inspection can lead to different outcomes depending on what is found. The findings may lead to:

Removing or fishing out a foreign object
Addressing a specific physical problem
Considering borehole rehabilitation
Assessing the pump or other equipment
Carrying out further assessment
Taking no immediate action

An inspection does not automatically mean that your borehole needs repair or rehabilitation. The appropriate response depends on the findings and the problem being investigated.
