Why Borehole Rehabilitation Matters

Boreholes can lose performance over time, even when properly constructed and maintained. This is a common outcome of ordinary use, not usually a sign that the borehole has failed.

Over time, conditions beneath the ground can change. Mineral deposits can build up, sediment can accumulate, and components within the borehole can gradually become less efficient. These changes can reduce water yield or affect water quality. In some cases, similar symptoms may also be caused by problems with the pumping system, which is why assessment comes first.

Where the underlying cause is treatable, rehabilitation can restore a borehole's performance without the cost of drilling a new one.

An underperforming borehole is not necessarily a failed borehole. Restoring the one you have protects the investment already made in it.

Is Borehole Rehabilitation Right for Your Borehole?

Not every underperforming borehole is a good candidate for rehabilitation. Whether it is depends on the borehole's condition and the specific cause of the performance problem.

Assessment may include checking whether the water level recovers after the borehole has been left at rest for a period, together with other diagnostic findings, to determine whether rehabilitation is likely to help. This distinguishes problems that rehabilitation can resolve from those that call for a different solution.

Our recommendations follow from what the assessment finds, not from assumptions about the borehole's condition. If rehabilitation is not the appropriate solution for your borehole, we will explain why and recommend the most suitable alternative.

What Happens During Borehole Rehabilitation?

Once you contact us, the process moves through the following stages.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    We review your borehole's records, where available, and inspect its current condition.

  2. 2

    Diagnosis

    We identify the cause of reduced performance, including checking whether the water level recovers after a rest period, and determine whether the issue lies in the borehole, the pumping system, or both.

  3. 3

    Method Selection

    Based on the diagnosis, we determine the rehabilitation methods required for your borehole.

  4. 4

    Rehabilitation Work

    Our team carries out the required work on site.

  5. 5

    Testing

    We test the borehole to confirm that rehabilitation has improved its performance and that it is operating as expected.

  6. 6

    Findings and Recommendations

    We explain what the assessment and testing found, together with any recommendations.

  7. 7

    Handover

    We provide guidance on operating and caring for your borehole to help maintain reliable performance.

What Affects the Cost of Borehole Rehabilitation?

Every rehabilitation project differs, and the cost depends on the condition of your borehole and the work required to restore its performance. Factors that influence cost include:

Borehole Depth

Deeper boreholes generally require more time and specialised equipment to work on.

Nature of the Problem

Less extensive rehabilitation generally costs less than projects involving casing repair, screen replacement, or more extensive treatment.

Rehabilitation Methods Required

The combination of methods needed depends on what the diagnosis identifies.

Pump Condition

Where the pump also requires servicing or replacement, this affects the overall scope of work.

Site Access

Remote or difficult-to-reach sites can increase mobilisation time and costs.

Regulatory Approvals

Work that includes deepening or widening your borehole requires prior approval, which can affect both project cost and timeline.

Because these factors vary from one project to another, an accurate quotation can only be prepared after assessing your borehole.
