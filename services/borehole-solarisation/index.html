Why Borehole Solarisation Matters

A borehole solar system involves more than adding panels to an existing setup. It has to be matched to your borehole's depth, the rating of the submersible pump and motor, the amount of water your borehole can reliably provide, the sunlight available at your site, and the amount of water you need each day.

A poorly matched system can leave pumps underpowered in low light, storage unable to meet demand, or your home, farm, business, or institution without enough water when it is needed most. A properly matched system can power your borehole reliably for years with minimal intervention.

For this reason, solarisation is a system we design around your project, not a product we sell as a standard offering.

Is Borehole Solarisation Right for Your Project?

Solar is suitable for many boreholes, but not all.

Whether it is the right option depends on factors such as the pumping depth, the amount of water required each day, the space available at your site for installing the solar panels, and the overall demands the system will place on the solar equipment.

Establishing whether solar is the right fit requires looking at these factors together, since a project can meet one comfortably while falling short on another.

That's why the first question is not which solar equipment to install. It's whether solar is the right solution for your project.

We assess these factors before making a recommendation. If solar is suitable, we'll recommend a system designed around the amount of water you need each day. If it is not, we'll explain why and recommend the most appropriate alternative, which may include a hybrid system that combines solar with another power source.

What Happens During Borehole Solarisation?

Every borehole solarisation project follows a series of carefully planned stages:

  1. 1

    Site and Borehole Assessment

    We review your borehole's technical details and your site's conditions, including the sunlight available throughout the day.

  2. 2

    Water Requirement Review

    Next, we establish how much water you need and when you need it, so the system reflects your actual requirements rather than a general estimate.

  3. 3

    System Design

    Using this information, we determine the pump, solar array, and storage configuration that's suited to your borehole, your site, and your water requirements.

  4. 4

    Installation

    Our team installs the system and tests it to confirm it's operating correctly before handover.

  5. 5

    Handover

    Finally, we explain how the system operates and provide guidance on using and maintaining it for reliable long-term performance.

What Affects the Cost of Borehole Solarisation?

Because every solar system is designed around the specific project, the cost of borehole solarisation varies from one project to another. Rather than provide a single figure that may not reflect your project, we explain the main factors that influence cost so you know what to expect before requesting a quotation.

Pumping Depth

Deeper boreholes generally require more powerful pumps and larger solar arrays.

Daily Water Requirements

The amount of water you need each day and the distance the water needs to travel from the borehole influence the size and capacity of the solar system required. Depending on your water requirements, additional water storage may also be needed, which can increase the overall cost of the system.

Site Conditions

The amount of available sunlight and your site's layout influence the size and positioning of the solar array.

Existing Borehole System

Whether your borehole is already equipped, or requires equipping alongside solarisation, affects the scope of work and the overall installation cost.

After assessing your borehole, your site, and your water requirements, we'll prepare a quotation that's tailored to your project and the solar system that's right for it.
